From's Gambit

Black gives away two pawns. Grab them carelessly and it's mate on move six.

In plain words

White opens with the f-pawn and Black gives away two pawns. With the f-pawn gone, the road from h4 to White's king is open. If White ignores it, the black queen checks on h4 and it's checkmate two moves later. Bring your knight to f3 first: it guards h4.

The trap

How the From's Gambit works

After 1.f4 e5 2.fxe5 d6 3.exd6 Bxd6 the bishop aims at g3 and the queen can check on h4. The natural 4.Nc3?? allows 4…Qh4+ 5.g3 Qxg3+ 6.hxg3 Bxg3#. 4.Nf3! guards h4 and White keeps a good game.

How not to fall for it

  1. Play 4.Nf3 first. The knight guards h4 and White keeps a good game.
  2. After you push your f-pawn, watch the road from h4 to your king.

Step by step

How to beat the From's Gambit

a good move for youa threat to watch

Two pawns, one open road

You took two pawns and Black took one back. But your f-pawn is gone, so the queen can check on h4, and the bishop aims at g3.

The fix: knight to f3

Bring your knight to f3. It guards h4, so the queen check is gone. You stay a pawn up with a good game.

The mistake: ignoring h4

After knight c3 the queen checks on h4, and only the g-pawn can block.

Pawn moves like d4 or e4 don't help either: the same queen check wins material.

How it ends

The queen takes on g3, the pawn has to take back, and the bishop takes on g3. Checkmate on move six.

Questions and answers

How do I avoid the From's Gambit trap?

Play your knight to f3 on move four. It guards h4.

Who was Martin From?

A Danish chess player (1828-1895). He played this gambit in Copenhagen in 1862, and it is named after him.

What is the lesson?

After you push your f-pawn, watch the road from h4 to your king.
